Preventive Health Screening in Australia

An evaluation of population-level participation, demographic disparities, and regional accessibility across the National Bowel, Breast, and Cervical Screening Programs.

1. Annual Participation Over the Years

Longitudinal tracking of national screening participation rates from 2018 to 2023 across preventive health programs.

Cervical screening rebounded after the 2020 dip

Cervical participation fell from 61.5% in 2019 to 56.4% in 2020, before climbing to 68.7% by 2022. Bowel and breast screening followed different paths across 2018 to 2023, so each program has its own distinct trajectory.

2. Bowel Cancer Screening

Detailed examination of the National Bowel Cancer Screening Program across sexes, age groups, and Primary Health Networks.

Women lead bowel screening participation

In 2020–2021, participation among ages 50–74 was 42.8% for women and 38.9% for men, a 3.9 percentage-point gap. Women also led in all five age bands shown, so the difference is consistent, not a one-off result.

Local participation varies widely across Australia

Across 337 SA3 areas with numeric rates, participation ranges from 9.1% at Christmas Island to 53.6% in Surf Coast–Bellarine Peninsula, a wide 44.5-point spread. That gap matters when targeting outreach, but the map shows where, not why.

Regional health networks tell different stories

Across 31 PHNs, bowel participation ranges from 25.3% in the Northern Territory to 47.3% in Gippsland. Even the highest network remains below half, so every region has room to improve, and some have much further to go.

3. Breast Cancer Screening

Evaluating eligible population volumes, age participation peaks, and geographic drive-time accessibility.

Eligible populations differ greatly by state

New South Wales has about 4.52 million eligible women, while the Northern Territory has about 110,000. Population size sets the scale of potential screening demand. These counts show who could be screened, not who actually attended.

Participation peaks in the late sixties

In 2021–2022, female participation rises from 45.4% at ages 50–54 to a peak of 54.5% at ages 65–69, then eases to 52.0% at ages 70–74. The youngest eligible group has the lowest rate of those shown.

Distance is a Northern Territory challenge

With services permanently open, 36.9% of Northern Territory’s eligible women live in SA3s more than 120 minutes from a service, versus just 0.1% in Victoria. Geography shapes who can realistically reach a service, so access matters for planning.

4. Cervical Cancer Screening

Median waiting times from screening to colposcopy and participation rates over time.

Population makeup differs sharply between states

First Nations women make up 24.7% of the combined First Nations and non-Indigenous eligible women in the Northern Territory, compared with 0.7% in Victoria. This shows who lives there, not screening behaviour, so it guides equitable planning.

Outreach needs differ from region to region

Participation benchmarks differ noticeably between regions, and follow-up referral rates vary too. Seeing these gaps side by side helps decision-makers direct outreach and resources to the places where support is needed.

Screening outcomes shifted between reporting periods

Test positivity and recall requirements are tracked across reporting periods, showing whether more people are being sent for further checks over time. Participation is only the start, and these outcomes complete the picture of screening impact.

5. Diagnostic Outcome of Preventive Screening

Clinical pathway analysis mapping active screening participation flows directly to definitive diagnostic outcomes.

Screening pathways lead to different outcomes

Participants in the bowel, breast and cervical programs flow towards three outcomes: negative, further assessment or positive. Each program ends differently, so tracking pathways shows how many people move forward and what happens to them next.
