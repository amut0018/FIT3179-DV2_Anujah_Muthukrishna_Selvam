Preventive Health Screening in Australia

An evaluation of population-level participation, demographic disparities, and regional accessibility across the National Bowel, BreastScreen, and Cervical Screening Programs.

1. National Screening Activity & Scale

Over the last decade, Australia's population-based screening frameworks have undergone technological shifts and external disruptions, notably the COVID-19 pandemic and the renewal of the National Cervical Screening Program.

Pandemic Shock & Diagnostic Recovery

Quarterly testing capacity experienced significant disruption during early 2020. BreastScreen services briefly suspended routine screenings in Q2 2020 to divert acute clinical resources and reduce infection vectors.

Conversely, the National Bowel Cancer Screening Program (NBCSP) retained steadier volumes due to its distributed at-home immunochemical faecal occult blood test (iFOBT) kit model.

-38% Decline in BreastScreen mammograms during Q2 2020

The Gender Participation Gap

National participation rates vary substantially across programs and sexes. In the bowel screening program, eligible women consistently screen at a higher rate than eligible men, despite incidence being slightly higher in males.

Across all programs, participation sits below the AIHW aspirational benchmark of 50%, highlighting ongoing opportunities for targeted public health messaging.
