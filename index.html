National Health Surveillance

Preventive Health Screening in Australia

An evaluation of population-level participation, demographic disparities, and regional accessibility across the National Bowel, BreastScreen, and Cervical Screening Programs.

1. National activity and participation

Annual screening activity and participation rates reveal how outcomes differ across programs, age groups, and sexes.

Cervical activity fell most in 2020

Between 2019 and 2020, the annual cervical screening-test total fell 56.8% (1.54 million to 667,481), compared with a 15.5% fall in Breast screening visits and 4.8% in returned bowel kits. The larger shift highlights how differently program activity changed over the same period. 2023 shows only January–March in the source data.

Women’s bowel participation was higher

In 2020–2021, national bowel-screening participation for ages 50–74 was 42.8% for females and 38.9% for males, a 3.9 percentage-point gap. Female rates were higher in each of the five age bands shown, making the overall difference consistent across the displayed age range.

2. Regional patterns and population differences

State, SA3, and Primary Health Network results show how participation, eligible population, and age-related rates vary across Australia.

SA3 participation spans a wide range

Among the 337 SA3 areas with numeric rates, participation ranges from 9.1% at Christmas Island to 53.6% in Surf Coast–Bellarine Peninsula, a 44.5-point spread. Such local variation matters when targeting outreach; the map shows where rates differ, not why.

Participation also varies between PHNs

In the 31 PHNs shown, bowel participation ranges from 25.3% in the Northern Territory to 47.3% in Gippsland. Even the highest PHN rate is below half, while the spread points to substantial differences between regional screening populations.

Eligible BreastScreen populations differ in size

The dot map represents 200 eligible women per dot. Eastern Melbourne, the largest PHN cohort shown, has 219,733 eligible women (about 1,098 dots); Western Queensland has 8,080 (40 dots). These counts describe the eligible population, not how many women attended screening.

Breast participation peaks at ages 65–69

Female participation in 2021–2022 rises from 45.4% at ages 50–54 to a peak of 54.5% at ages 65–69, then eases to 52.0% at ages 70–74. The younger eligible age band has the lowest rate among those shown.

3. Access and equity

Travel distance and the makeup of eligible populations add context for planning screening services that are reachable and inclusive.

Long travel distances are concentrated in the NT

For the permanently open service scenario, 36.9% of the Northern Territory’s eligible women are in SA3s more than 120 minutes from a service, versus 0.1% in Victoria. The state-level contrast highlights geographic access as an important part of screening-service planning.

Eligible population composition differs by state

First Nations women make up 24.7% of the combined First Nations and non-Indigenous eligible-women total in the Northern Territory, compared with 0.7% in Victoria. This chart shows population composition—not screening participation—so it informs equitable planning rather than indicating a difference in screening behaviour.
